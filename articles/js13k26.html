I've been quite passionate, but my biggest special interest by far this year has been... WORDS !

More precisely, I wanted to gather good french and english dictionaries, see how small I could compress them, and eventually use it in JS games... which led to the creation of the following projects:

  • Words is a webpage displaying 3 different french and english dictionaries in many formats and with a lot of options (Github - Website)
  • MiniPrefixRemover is a first attempt at compressing an array of words by removing repeated prefixes (Github - Website)
  • Boggle is a Boggle grid generator and solver based on the "words" Scrabble dictionaries (Github - Website)
  • Word Games is my bonus GamedevJS 2026 entry, coded in 24 hours! (Github - Website)

And then, I spent the last 6 months brainstorming almost daily with ChatGPT (until it asked me to pay), then Claude (until it asked me to pay), then Gemini (who never asked me to pay), in one unique goal: find a way to compress a list of words as strongly as possible, in the wild hope to create the first js13kGames entry embedding a full english dictionary!

First, with "words", "MiniPrefixRemover" and "RoadRoller" in hand, I identified the best candidate for my (maybe future) "js13k dictionary":

Let's take all English Scrabble words, up to 5 letters long. That's 14,915 words, weighing 109kb in JSON format or 80kb in a comma-separated list.

Zipping the list leads to a 24kb zip file... or 14kb if we add a strong pass of RoadRoller. Super impressive, but still too big.

Now, let's try again with MiniPrefixRemover, then RoadRoller, then zip: we're now under 13kb (12,741b)! That's nice, but not enough to include a word game, or a collection of word games similar to my GamedevJS entry...

So the question is: how low can we go? And that's the topic of my long discussion with AI agents.

How long, you ask? Well, just to give you an idea, I copy-pasted all my exchanges in a Word document in font-size 12pt, and it exceeded 1500 pages.

Here's another way to see it: a video showing my 6-month conversation (in french) with 3 AIs:

How I discussed with these AIs:

Every time I had a (more or less crazy) idea to compress my words, I explained that idea to the AI and asked it to code a little "HTML lab": a webpage that loads the dictionary, applies all the transformations I could think of, roadroll and zip the result, and verify if the encoded data is 1) decodable without ambiguity, and 2) smaller that our current record.

I'm pretty sure we covered virtually every possible approach for compressing this dictionary, from woff2 to to arithmetic encoding, with detours towards things called trie, RLE, SSE, ZSDT, DAWG, BWT, bigrams, trigrams, lexical pruning, column delta, and many others that I forgot.

The main thing that I retained from all these approaches is that they introduced entropy in my data, making it actually less RoadRoller-friendly and less Zip-friendly.

Since RoadRoller and Zip love low-entropy (repeated and/or easily predictable) data, the solution was to transform my dictionary juuuuust a little: nudging things here and there, introducing a few special characters to help eliminate plenty of redundant information, but never, never making the data less predictable by trying to be "clever". When I understood that, my seemingly "dumb" suggestions finally managed to reduce my zip's size!

Starting from MiniPrefixRemover, the 5 best compressor improvements that we made during this long brainstorming were saved in my Wordcompression Github repo.

It's important to note that the AIs were able to suggest well-known compression algorithms, but the "recipes" used in the end were 100% dictated by me. The AIs were just there to formalize my ideas, implement the HTML labs for me, and explain to me why some things worked and some other things didn't work.

And how about the dictionary size? Well, on july 27, I ended up reducing it under 11kb (11,266 bytes), thanks to the compressor called "omit2.html".
